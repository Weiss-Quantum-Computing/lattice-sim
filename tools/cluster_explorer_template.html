    Stabilizer measurement

    Atoms of one sublattice are detected in their stabilizer letter, the other sublattice in Z; a stabilizer is recorded when every atom it acts on was detected in its letter. Values are relative to the ideal sign. Uses the sequence up to its first detection or push-out.

    Model

    Gate. In a leg, the |3,+m⟩ component of the atom at r moves +ê and meets the |3,−m⟩ component of the atom at r + ê, so |q_r = 1, q_s = 0⟩ gains e−iφ (|4,+1⟩ maps to the +ê mover; unconfirmed, it changes stabilizer signs only). With the π pulse between legs the bond gate is exp(+iφ/2 ZZ), followed by X on every atom: φ = π/2 per leg gives √ZZ† (Du Sec. 4.2.2.3). A single leg is Clifford only for φ = π. All atoms receive the global pulses, including isolated ones.

    Stabilizer letter. After the √X start, Si = ±Pi∏Znb with P = X for an odd number of collided neighbours and Y for an even number. Du (p. 57) lists the opposite letters, which belong to a |+⟩ start (Ry(π/2) first pulse). Z(π/2) on the even-degree atoms therefore turns every letter into X, and the two configurations XZ-A and XZ-B then record all stabilizers. A vacancy or an uncleared peripheral atom changes the parity of each of its neighbours and flips their letters.

    Noise. Echo contrast C per collision step enters as a Z error (1 − C)/2 on every atom. A pair-phase error δ ~ N(offset, σ) enters as a ZZ error ⟨sin²(δ/4)⟩ per bond. Spin-exchange leakage enters as two-qubit depolarizing noise. The fidelity shown is exact for this Pauli model (the probability that the accumulated error flips no stabilizer); the bound 1 − ½Σ(1 − ⟨Si⟩) holds for any state.
